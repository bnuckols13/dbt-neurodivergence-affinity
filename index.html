Affinity Group — Adult ED Recovery

Neurodivergence in Recovery

A one-off virtual space for ND clients to be in a room together, on our own terms.

Today

What this is 8 to 10 min

This is an affinity group, not a process group and not a skills group. One hour for people in this program who identify as neurodivergent, however you arrived at that word. Diagnosed, self-identified, exploring the question, all welcome.

There is no curriculum to get through. No homework. No charting of what gets said unless someone names a safety concern, and you will hear it named if that happens.

Confidentiality

What's shared here stays here. Names, stories, details, all of it is protected. The only exception is safety.

You can pass at any point. No explanation needed. If something doesn't fit right now, say "pass" and we move on.

How this room works

Virtual ground rules 3 min

Cameras are optional. The chat is a full channel, not a backup. Speaking, typing, reacting, or sitting silently all count as being here. Stim, fidget, knit, look away from the screen, lie on the floor. None of it needs explanation.

Access in chat

If you want, drop your name and pronouns in chat, plus one access need for the hour ("please repeat the prompt," "going off-camera," "I'll respond in chat only"). Optional. The facilitator will go first.

Opening

One word in chat 3 min

When you're ready, drop one word in chat for how you arrived. No elaboration required. The facilitator reads them aloud, slowly, with pauses. That is the opener.

Shared vocabulary

A few words, lightly offered 10 to 12 min

These are concepts that often help name what people in this group already know in their bodies. Open the ones that interest you. Skip the rest. The facilitator will name each briefly, then open the floor.

Double empathy
+

The communication gap between neurodivergent and neurotypical people runs both directions. Years of feeling "too much" or "not enough" in process groups may be a mismatch between parties, not a deficit in one of them.

Milton, 2012.
Masking and unmasking
+

The conscious and unconscious work of suppressing ND traits to pass. The cost shows up as exhaustion, lost self-knowledge, and burnout. Unmasking is not a performance of ND traits. It is the slow return of access to your own preferences and signal.

Monotropism
+

Attention pools deeply into a narrow set of interests rather than distributing across many. This is why interruption costs so much, why transitions are hard, why "just stop and come back later" doesn't translate.

Spiky profile
+

Abilities are uneven. The peaks and valleys do not predict each other. Treatment often reads the valleys as resistance and the peaks as evidence you should be able to do everything else.

Interoception, alexithymia
+

ND bodies often send unreliable or differently-calibrated internal signals. Hunger, fullness, fatigue, emotion, all of it can be hard to read. "Listen to your body" assumes a signal that is steady. For many of us, the signal is not steady, and the work is different.

Autistic burnout, RSD
+

Burnout is the chronic exhaustion and skill-loss after sustained masking or demand-overload. It looks like depression, lives in the nervous system. RSD names the speed and intensity of the pain that comes with perceived rejection or criticism, common in ADHD presentations.

None of this is a test. Take what fits, leave what doesn't. The point is to have words available if you reach for them later.

Bridge

From ND experience to recovery 10 to 12 min

Three questions. Whoever wants to come in, comes in. Speak, type in chat, or pass. Silence between answers is not a problem to solve.

1
When did food first become complicated in a way that wasn't about weight or shape?
Texture, smell, predictability, the wrong fork, eating in front of people, any of it counts. Specifics over summaries.
2
What's one thing you've been masking this week that you'd set down if you could?
Eye contact, tone of voice, sitting still, performing engagement in groups. Name the specific thing, not the category.
3
Is there a word for your experience you found recently that fit?
A diagnosis, a community term, a metaphor. Or none of the above and that's the answer.
Facilitator note

Resist filling the silence. Pauses are processing time. If the same voice comes in twice, name the room: "I want to make sure there's space for other voices, and no pressure if you don't want to come in."

The heart of the hour

Group discussion 18 to 22 min

Pick the prompts that have life in the room. Not all of them. The facilitator offers one, holds the silence, lets the conversation go where it goes, then offers the next when energy fades.

Prompt 1
What gets read as ED behavior in this program that is actually ND for you?
Sensory food refusal, rigid routines, parallel eating, hyperfocus through meals, the same breakfast every day. Validate without competing on whose example is "more" ND.
Prompt 2
Name a food rule that is actually a sensory rule.
Often a relief to say out loud. Hold the difference between rule-as-restriction and rule-as-regulation. Both can be true at once.
Prompt 3
What does masking cost you in treatment specifically?
Groups, meals, milieu, interactions with staff. The honest answer often surprises the room. Do not redirect to silver linings.
Prompt 4
What accommodation do you wish this program had?
Concrete is the point. Lower lights at dinner, written check-ins, advance notice on schedule changes, fewer surprise process groups, a quiet room. Encourage the room to riff off each other.
Prompt 5 — optional
What special interest or deep focus has kept you alive?
Closer to the bone. Use only if the room has settled. Often produces the most connection of the hour.
From this hour

Pick one to take with you 5 to 7 min

Not a homework assignment. A small handhold for the rest of the day. Pick the one that has the least friction.

One unmasking micro-move
Stim openly in one group this week. Skip the eye contact you usually fake. Let your voice land where it lands.
One accommodation to ask for
Name a person and a request. "I'm going to ask my therapist for written check-ins." Concrete beats general.
One ND-affirming resource
Stimpunks, Therapist Neurodiversity Collective, Reframing Autism, the Monotropism Questionnaire, Devon Price's Unmasking Autism.
Nothing
Showing up was the work. That counts. Closing the laptop and resting is a complete answer.
Checkout

One sentence, in chat or aloud 3 min

One thing you're taking from this hour, plus the small concrete thing you'll do or not do because of it. One sentence, not a paragraph. Chat is fine. Pass is fine.

Facilitator note

Push gently for specifics. "I'll rest" becomes "I'll close the laptop for twenty minutes after this." Then close the room with thirty seconds of shared silence and a clear ending. ND nervous systems regulate better with a known landing.
